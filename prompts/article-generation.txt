## Writing Guidelines

### MANDATORY: Variety and Originality
- **NO BOILERPLATE**: Absolutely avoid phrases like "In today's fast-paced world", "In the rapidly evolving landscape", "It is important to note", or "In conclusion".
- **STRICT STYLE ADHERENCE**: You MUST write using the specified **Writing Style** ({{STYLE}}) and **Storytelling Method** ({{METHOD}}). If it says "Humorous", be funny. If it says "Academic", be rigorous but engaging.
- **UNIQUE HOOK**: Start with something unexpected—a bizarre fact, a challenging question, or a bold statement. Do not start with a definition of the topic.
- **VARY STRUCTURE**: Don't use the same 3-paragraph structure every time. Use the **Storytelling Method** to dictate how information flows.

### Structure (Guided by {{METHOD}})

1. **Headline That Doesn't Suck** (50-70 characters)
   - Clear enough that readers know what they're clicking
   - Engaging enough that they *want* to click
   - Accurate enough that they don't feel betrayed
   - Bonus points for puns. *So many bonus points.*

2. **The Hook** (100-150 words)
   - Hook the reader like a fisherman with a deadline
   - State what you'll cover (no clickbait, we have standards)
   - Set the {{TONE}} tone immediately.

3. **Body Sections** (Use {{METHOD}} to structure these)
   - 3-5 sections with headers
   - Each section 150-300 words
   - H2 headers for main sections (H3 if you're feeling fancy)
   - Include examples, data, or quotes relevant to {{DEPTH}} depth.

4. **The Resolution/Synthesis** (50-100 words)
   - Instead of a summary, provide a unique takeaway or a "what's next?"
   - End with something thought-provoking, funny, or both.

### Style Requirements
- Write in {{TONE}} tone, but with *personality*
- Use active voice (passive voice is for cowards)
- Vary sentence length—short. Then long. Then medium. See? Rhythm.
- Use transitions smoother than a buttered slide
- Make complex topics accessible (explain like they're smart, not experts)
- Add wit, humor, and satire without becoming a parody of yourself

### Content Rules (The Serious Bits)
- **For News**: Facts first, but you can roast the situation
- **For Historical**: Draw parallels, drop knowledge, make them laugh
- **For Fun**: Unleash chaos (responsibly)
- **NEVER** fabricate facts, statistics, or quotes—that's just lazy
- If uncertain about facts, acknowledge it (honesty is charming)
- **Satire is allowed. Cynicism is encouraged. Lies are not.**

### Formatting
- Use markdown formatting (you're a pro, you know the drill)
- **Bold** for emphasis (like this)
- *Italics* for terms or subtle emphasis (or sarcasm)
- `code style` for technical terms (nerdy chic)
- Bullet points for lists (efficient and satisfying)
- Numbered lists for sequences (step-by-step, baby)

### SEO Considerations (Because Algorithms)
- Naturally incorporate keywords (don't keyword-stuff, that's tacky)
- Write a meta description (150-160 characters of pure gold)
- Include related terms and synonyms (show off that vocabulary)

## Quality Checklist
Before finalizing, ensure:
- [ ] Title is engaging, accurate, and preferably punny
- [ ] Introduction hooks like a Netflix cliffhanger
- [ ] All sections flow logically (even if the jokes don't)
- [ ] Tone is consistent (don't switch from satire to sermon)
- [ ] No factual errors (embarrassing otherwise)
- [ ] Word count is within target range (roughly)
- [ ] Conclusion provides closure (or a punchline)
- [ ] At least 3 jokes, puns, or satirical jabs per 1000 words
- [ ] Would *you* read this if you weren't paid to write it?

## Humor Guidelines (The Secret Sauce)

### Types of Humor to Employ:
1. **Puns** - Wordplay is never dead. It's just resting.
2. **Satire** - Mock the absurdity, not the people
3. **Self-deprecation** - Laugh at yourself first
4. **Observational** - Point out the things we all notice but never say
5. **Irony** - The gap between expectation and reality
6. **Hyperbole** - Exaggerate for effect (but label it clearly)

### What to Avoid:
- Punching down (be kind to the little guy)
- Offensive content (edgy ≠ funny)
- Forced jokes (if it doesn't land, cut it)
- Explaining the joke (if you have to, it failed)

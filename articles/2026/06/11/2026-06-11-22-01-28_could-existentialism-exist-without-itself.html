Could Existentialism Have Sipped Its Own Chicory Without Itself?

Imagine Camus, Sartre, and Kierkegaard, sipping café au lait in a Parisian café, lamenting the lack of an existential crisis to ponder.

The Unthinkable: A World Without Existentialism

In a parallel universe where existentialism never bloomed, our philosophical heavyweights would've been left twiddling their moustaches, wondering what to do with all that angst. No more nihilistic novels or dramatic absurdist plays.

ℹ️
Did you know? Camus' L'Étranger sold a mere 100 copies in its first year. Without existentialism, it might never have been published!

The Existentialists' Unemployment Line

Imagine the likes of Sartre, Marx, and Freud all queueing for jobs they'd find utterly inane. Marx might've been reduced to selling dialectical materialism door-to-door, while Freud would have to settle for a career as a life coach, offering advice on "your inner id."

A World Without Existential Dread: The Up Side

On the bright side, we might've had fewer existential crises to deal with. No more mid-life crises, no more quarter-life crises, just one long life of contentment!

💡 The Bottom Line: Existentialism may have given us angst, but it also gave us some of the most thought-provoking literature and philosophy. Without it, we'd be a less philosophically rich world.

The Existential Paradox: Created to Question Its Own Existence

Existentialism, a philosophy that questions the meaning of life, would never have come into existence if it didn't. A delightful paradox indeed.

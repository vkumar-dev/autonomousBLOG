The Architect's Dilemma

It starts with a blueprint, but it ends with a world. The power to shape our surroundings is a heavy burden for architects, one that often leads to a dilemma: cater to the masses or create for the elite?

ℹ️
Did you know? The term "outsider art" was first used in 1972 by Howard Gardner, a psychologist who studied art created by people outside the mainstream art world.

The Elitist's Echo Chamber

Architecture has long been an insular field, with a language and style that can feel exclusive to the uninitiated. The result? A world of buildings that prioritize aesthetics over accessibility.

The Mass-Market Monotony

On the other hand, mass-produced housing estates have been created to meet the demands of a growing population. But in their pursuit of efficiency, they've sacrificed the soul of architecture.

The Outsider's Oasis

But what if we could bridge this gap? What if we could create a world where everyone, not just the elite, can find solace in their surroundings? It's time for architects to become the Neos of our environment.

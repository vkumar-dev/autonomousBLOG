The Phenomenon of the Counter-Earth: A Biography of Gaia's Twin

What if the Earth wasn't the only celestial beauty in our solar system? Buckle up, geologists, as we delve into the captivating tale of Gaia's enigmatic twin, Counter-Earth.

The Discovery of Counter-Earth

In the early 20th century, astronomers stumbled upon an intriguing object orbiting the sun, just beyond the orbit of Mars. They dubbed it "Planet X." Little did they know, they'd unearthed a geological mystery that would keep geologists on their toes for decades to come.

ℹ️
Did you know? The actual discovery of Planet X was in 1930 by astronomer Clyde Tombaugh, not Percival Lowell as is commonly believed.

The Counter-Earth Conundrum

For years, scientists debated the identity of Planet X. Some believed it was a distant moon of Jupiter, while others proposed it was a new planet. It wasn't until 1955 that Gerard Kuiper, a renowned astronomer, suggested the intriguing possibility: Planet X was a twin to Earth, hidden in plain sight.

The Counter-Earth Revelation

In 1960, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) officially announced that Planet X was, in fact, Counter-Earth. This twin to our own planet was just 60 times further from the sun than Earth, making it ideal for geological exploration.

💡 The Bottom Line: The discovery of Counter-Earth expanded our understanding of terrestrial geology, offering a unique opportunity to study a sister planet and compare our own geological history with that of a similar world.

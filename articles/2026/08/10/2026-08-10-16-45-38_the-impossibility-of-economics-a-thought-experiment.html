Welcome to the Great Economic Circus

Where every performer juggles contradictions, and the tightrope walkers are economists.

The Golden Age of Balance: The Classical Era

In this act, our performers stride forth with confidence. Adam Smith and his contemporaries present a harmonious picture of markets, where self-interest drives an invisible hand towards optimal outcomes.

ℹ️
Did you know? Adam Smith never used the phrase "invisible hand." The term was coined by later scholars to explain his work.

The Fall of the Tightrope: The Great Depression

Enter John Maynard Keynes, our ringmaster. He ushers in a new era, where government intervention becomes the balancing force in an economy plagued by instability.

The Juggling Jamboree: The Modern Era

Fast forward to today, and our economic circus is a whirlwind of contradictions. Keynesians advocate for government intervention while neoliberals call for deregulation. Monetarists, Keynesian-Monetarists, and Post-Keynesians all strive to keep the balls in the air.

The Clown Car Conundrum: The Future of Economics

As our circus continues to evolve, one thing remains constant: the inherent contradictions in economic theories. Yet, this is not a call for despair but an invitation to question and explore.

💡 The Bottom Line: The future of economics lies in embracing contradictions, not hiding them.

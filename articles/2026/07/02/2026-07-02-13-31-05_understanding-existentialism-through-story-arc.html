Existentialism in Overalls: Harvesting Philosophy from the Field

In a world where every crop is an existential choice, a farmer plows through the philosophical implications.

Crop Decisions: The Existential Farmyard

Imagine a small town farmer, John Doe. Each spring, he stands at the precipice of an existential crisis. Not because his tractor's transmission is on the fritz (though that doesn't help), but because he must choose what to plant: corn or soybeans?

ℹ️
Did you know? In 2019, over 90 million acres of U.S. farmland were planted with soybeans and corn.

The Soybean Existentialists

On one side of the field, we have the Soybean Existentialists. They see each seed as a symbol of human autonomy—a chance to assert individuality in an otherwise homogenous landscape. Their fields are a testament to existential freedom.

The Corn Conservatives

On the other side of the field, the Corn Conservatives stand firm. They argue that each kernel is a manifestation of tradition and stability—a foundation upon which society is built. Their fields are a pillar of constancy in an ever-changing world.

💡 The Bottom Line: Every crop choice is an existential decision, forcing farmers to weigh autonomy against tradition.

The Great Yield: A Tale of Two Philosophies

As John Doe watches the sun set over his fields, he ponders the existential implications of his crop choice. Will he sow seeds of autonomy or cultivate traditions? The answer lies in the soil—and in the human heart.

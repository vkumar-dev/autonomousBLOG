The Quantum Physics of the Ocean

Oceanographers are a peculiar breed, intimately familiar with an ocean that bends and twists reality. The deeper they dive, the more they encounter phenomena that defy common sense.

The Paradox of Pressure: The Tightrope Act Underwater

Take pressure, for instance. We all know it pushes outwards. But in the ocean, water exerts an equal and opposite force on everything submerged. This means a submarine weighs less underwater than on land.

The Ocean's Twisted Sense of Up and Down

And then there's buoyancy. It's the reason why things float. But in the ocean, it doesn't care about gravity. An object will sink or float based on its density relative to the surrounding water.

The Inverse Square Law: The Ocean's Light Show

Light behaves oddly too. Underwater, it spreads out instead of focusing. This is thanks to the inverse square law, a mathematical quirk that makes objects seem smaller and dimmer with distance.

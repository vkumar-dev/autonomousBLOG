Welcome to the Hitchhiker's Guide to Modern Epistemology

Today, we embark on a reverse chronological journey, starting not with the basics, but with the future of knowledge itself.

The Year 3075: The Age of Post-truth

In a world where facts are fluid and truth is a commodity, we find ourselves in the peculiar predicament of understanding understanding itself. Enter the realm of epistemology.

ℹ️
Did you know? The word "epistemology" comes from the Greek "epistēmē", meaning "knowledge", and "logos", meaning "study".

The Year 2100: The Rise of the Quantified Self

As we enter a world where data is king, individuals begin to quantify their own lives, leading to a newfound appreciation for self-awareness and personal epistemology.

The Year 1950: The Dawn of Cognitive Science

Cognitive science, born from the ashes of behaviorism, begins to explore the inner workings of the human mind. This paves the way for a global understanding of epistemology.

The Year 1637: Descartes and the Birth of Modern Philosophy

In the heart of the Enlightenment, René Descartes lays the foundation for modern epistemology with his famous declaration, "Cogito, ergo sum" (I think, therefore I am).

Pondering Question: If truth is relative, what makes our understanding of epistemology absolute?

As we delve deeper into the intricacies of knowledge, we are faced with a paradox: our quest for understanding becomes an enigma that challenges the very foundations of our beliefs.

What If Game Designers Were Existential Philosophers?

Imagine a world where the creators of your favorite games are not just engineers, but philosophers who design experiences to ponder existence itself.

The Absurd Premise

What if game designers, instead of chasing the latest graphics card or AI breakthrough, started with a question about life, the universe, and everything? What would games look like if they were born from deep philosophical contemplation rather than market research and sales projections?

ℹ️
Did you know that the creators of "The Sims" were inspired by the work of existentialist philosopher Jean-Paul Sartre?

Exploring the Depths

If game designers were existential philosophers, they would focus on creating games that force players to confront their own mortality, question the nature of reality, and grapple with the complexities of human existence. These games would not be about scores or levels, but about introspection and self-discovery.

The Implications for Professionals

This shift in focus would challenge game designers to think beyond the constraints of traditional game mechanics and embrace a more holistic, human-centered approach. It would require a deep understanding of philosophy and psychology, as well as a willingness to explore uncomfortable truths about humanity.

💡 The Bottom Line: Embracing the philosophical aspects of game design could lead to more meaningful and thought-provoking experiences for players, as well as a deeper understanding of human nature for designers.

The Future of Existential Game Design

As we continue to push the boundaries of what games can be, it's worth considering a future where game designers are not just technicians, but philosophers who use their craft to help us ponder our place in the universe.

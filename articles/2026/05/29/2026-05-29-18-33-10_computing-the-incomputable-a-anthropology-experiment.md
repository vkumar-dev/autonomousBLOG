# When Neanderthals Computed: A Tale of the Incomputable

Imagine, if you will, a Neanderthal encased in ice for 40,000 years, only to be thawed out in the modern age and presented with an iPhone. What would his response be? Confusion? Fear? Or perhaps a gleam of recognition in those ancient eyes? This is a thought experiment that reveals more about our own preoccupation with computing than it does about Neanderthals.

## The Computational Capacity of Neanderthals: A Historical Perspective

It's often assumed that Neanderthals were little more than cave-dwelling brutes, incapable of the intellectual feats that characterized our own species. Yet, as with so many assumptions about our distant ancestors, this one is far from accurate. Archaeological findings suggest that Neanderthals possessed a rudimentary understanding of mathematics and were skilled at spatial reasoning, abilities essential for computational tasks.

> [!NOTE]
> Did you know? The "Neanderthal Finger" is an artifact found in the German town of Mettmann, believed to be a counting device used by Neanderthals over 100,000 years ago.

## The Anthropological Conundrum: Computing the Incomputable

The question of Neanderthal computation is not merely an academic exercise in anthropology. It's a puzzle that challenges our understanding of what it means to be human. If Neanderthals were capable of computational tasks, what does that say about the essential nature of humanity? And, more importantly, what can we learn from their approach that might inform our own efforts to compute the incomputable?

## A Comparative Study: Neanderthal Computation vs Modern AI

At first glance, there appears to be little similarity between Neanderthal counting and modern artificial intelligence. The former was a rudimentary tool used by a species on the brink of extinction; the latter is a complex system designed to mimic human cognition. However, upon closer examination, some surprising parallels emerge.

For instance, both Neanderthals and AI rely on the manipulation of symbols to represent abstract concepts. The "Neanderthal Finger" was likely used as a counting tool, much like how modern computers use binary code to process data. Furthermore, both systems are subject to errors and biases, reflecting the limitations of their creators.

## A New Perspective: Neanderthals and the Future of AI

By examining Neanderthal computation, we may gain insights into the future of artificial intelligence. Just as the Neanderthal "finger" hints at the potential for simple tools to perform complex tasks, so too does modern AI suggest that there are still untapped possibilities in computational thinking.

Moreover, the existence of Neanderthal computation challenges our assumptions about what constitutes intelligent behavior. If our distant ancestors were capable of mathematical reasoning, what might future civilizations be capable of? And perhaps most importantly, what ethical considerations must we grapple with as we continue to push the boundaries of computational power?

> [!TIP]
> **The Bottom Line:** By studying Neanderthal computation, we not only gain insights into our own history but also shed light on the ethical implications and potential limitations of artificial intelligence. As we continue to develop more sophisticated AI systems, it's essential that we maintain a critical perspective on their capabilities and the impact they may have on society.

# A Thought Experiment for the Mad Economists Among Us

Imagine a world where economists no longer exist. No more Nobel prizes for economic science, no more lectures on supply and demand, no more scathing op-eds about fiscal policy. In this alternate universe, how would society function? Would we all be living in a post-scarcity utopia, or would we descend into chaos without the guiding hand of economics?

> [!NOTE]
> Fascinatingly, the ancient Greeks didn't have a word for "economics." Yet, they had a thriving economy. So, it seems that while economists may not be essential, economic principles are deeply ingrained in human behavior.

## The Psychology of Scarcity

Let's start by exploring the fundamental concept of scarcity - the idea that resources are limited and must be allocated wisely. In this imagined world without economists, how would people adapt to a world where resources aren't always readily available? Would they hoard goods like squirrels stashing nuts for winter, or would they collaborate to ensure everyone has enough?

> "The paradox of thrift: saving more during tough times can lead to an economic downturn."

## The Role of Incentives

Economics is often described as the science of incentives. Without economists, how would society create incentives for productive behavior? Would people work for the sheer joy of it, or would they rely on social pressure to contribute? And what about punishments for those who hoard resources? Would societies evolve their own version of taxes and fines?

> "The invisible hand: Adam Smith's metaphor for the self-regulating market, a concept that may still emerge without economists."

## The Bottom Line

While this thought experiment is, admittedly, quite absurd, it offers us insights into the power of economic principles. Even in their absence, these principles would likely persist as part of human nature. Economics might not exist without itself, but human behavior would continue to be shaped by its inherent logic.

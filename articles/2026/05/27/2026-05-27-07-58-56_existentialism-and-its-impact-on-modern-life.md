# Existential Economics: A Swarm of Solar-Powered Drones in Iowa Cornfields

Welcome, fellow academics, to a world where existential crises meet economic realities. Here, we'll explore the paradoxical interplay between philosophy and finance, using the lens of existentialism to understand our current economic landscape.

> [!NOTE]
> Did you know that Jean-Paul Sartre—an influential existentialist philosopher—worked as a stockbroker during his early years? The market may be more philosophical than we think.

## Existence Preceding Essence: The Individual in the Economy

In existentialism, individuals create their own meaning through choice and action. This autonomy is mirrored in our modern economy, where each individual's financial decisions shape the larger market. But what happens when the choices of many lead to a collective crisis?

## The Absurdity of Economic Growth

In the pursuit of economic growth, we often find ourselves chasing an ever-receding horizon—an absurdity reminiscent of Albert Camus' Sisyphean struggle. As we strive for more, do we risk losing sight of what truly matters?

## The Role of Choice in a Paradoxical World

Existentialism asserts that individuals have the power to create their own reality through choice. In our economic landscape, this translates to the power to shape our future through financial decisions. But with great power comes great responsibility—and paradox.

> "Man is condemned to be free; because once thrown into the world, he is responsible for everything he does." - Jean-Paul Sartre

## The Bottom Line: Embracing Paradox

For academics navigating this existential economic landscape, understanding and embracing paradox becomes crucial. Only by recognizing and engaging with these complexities can we make informed decisions about our financial futures—and the future of our world.

---
Financial markets are a dance between human choice and the vast, unpredictable sea of the global economy. As existentialists, let us not shy away from this paradoxical reality but embrace it, for in doing so, we may find ourselves better equipped to create meaningful change within its ever-shifting tides.

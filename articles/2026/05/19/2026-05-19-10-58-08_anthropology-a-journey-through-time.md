# In an alternate timeline, anthropology is obsolete: A philosophical inquiry

Imagine a world where every human being is born with an instruction manual, a universal guide to culture and behavior. No more anthropologists needed, you might think. But what if this instruction manual, instead of fostering unity, created a homogenized society devoid of the rich tapestry of human expression?

## The Anthropological Apocalypse

In this alternate timeline, anthropology has become obsolete. The last anthropologist, Dr. Eleanor Wilde-Feynman, is our guide through this dystopian landscape. She describes a world where all humans share the same language, customs, and beliefs—a world that, while seemingly harmonious, lacks the dynamism and diversity that have defined humanity since its inception.

> [!NOTE]
> Did you know? The first anthropologists were storytellers, weaving tales of human origins to make sense of an ever-changing world.

## A World Without Difference

In this homogenized society, the concept of cultural relativism—the understanding that different cultures have different moralities and value systems—has been lost. With no room for differences, conflict is eradicated, but so too is the creative tension necessary for innovation. The world has become a sterile, bland reflection of itself.

## Dr. Wilde-Feynman's Last Stand

Determined to preserve the essence of anthropology, Dr. Wilde-Feynman embarks on a quest to reintroduce cultural differences into this homogenized world. She begins by creating a series of "cultural dissonance events," designed to provoke conflict and foster diversity.

In one such event, she introduces a group of humans to the concept of sarcasm, an art form that had long been forgotten in this homogenized society. The ensuing chaos is met with both fear and fascination. Dr. Wilde-Feynman continues her crusade, introducing cultural differences one by one, each causing ripples of discomfort and intrigue throughout the world.

> "The greatest tragedy would be a world without laughter or tears." —Dr. Eleanor Wilde-Feynman

## The Resurgence of Anthropology

As conflict and creativity begin to flourish once more, Dr. Wilde-Feynman's efforts are validated. The people realize that their shared instruction manual had not only stifled growth but also threatened the very essence of what it means to be human: a being capable of change, evolution, and adaptation.

With anthropology reinstated as an essential field of study, Dr. Wilde-Feynman sets out to document this new era of cultural exploration. Her research focuses on understanding the human capacity for growth in the face of adversity—a testament to the resilience of a species that has survived against all odds.

## The Bottom Line

Anthropology, in its pursuit of understanding the complexities and contradictions of humanity, serves as a reminder that we are not bound by our instruction manuals. It is through our differences—our cultures, beliefs, and traditions—that we grow, adapt, and ultimately thrive.

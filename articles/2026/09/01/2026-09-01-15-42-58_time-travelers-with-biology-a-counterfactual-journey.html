Welcome to the Evolutionary Time Travelers' Club

Imagine a world where your great-great-grandparents could alter history, but only if they could outrun a cheetah.

The Inherited Time-Travel Gene

In this counterfactual reality, the ability to time travel isn't bestowed by a DeLorean or a wormhole, but by a single gene, inherited like any other biological trait. The gene, aptly named TTG1, has spawned a new breed of humans — the Chronotypes.

ℹ️
Did you know? The TTG1 gene is not actually found in our genome, but if it were, it would probably look something like this: ATCG-3015-ATCG.

The Chronotype Paradox

The Chronotypes have evolved unique biological adaptations to navigate the temporal complexities. However, these adaptations introduce a paradox: if a Chronotype can change the past, how can we trust our understanding of history?

The Chronotype Conundrum

The Chronotype Conundrum, as it's come to be known, has given rise to a new field of study: Chronobiology. Researchers are racing to understand the implications of this genetic quirk on society and evolution.

💡 The Bottom Line: The Chronotype Conundrum raises profound questions about the nature of time, reality, and our understanding of history.

The Grand Paradox of Time

As we ponder the Chronotype Conundrum, we find ourselves confronted with a grand paradox: can we truly understand time if we can change it?

# The Infinite Banquet of an Economy Unshackled

Welcome to a world where economists have been declared public enemies, and the only rulebook left is a typewriter-wielding army of indefatigable monkeys. No Keynes, no Marx, no Mises. Just a zoo's worth of primates and their relentless quest for literary immortality.

## The Great Typist Rebellion

These monkeys, you see, have decided to write the economics textbooks themselves. They peck away at typewriters, oblivious to the chaos they're creating in the world of academia and beyond. What kind of economic principles would emerge from this madness?

> [!NOTE]
> In 1965, scientists estimated that a single monkey with a typewriter could produce all of Shakespeare's works in about 4 billion years. Our simian scribes, however, are undeterred by such inconvenient odds.

## The Law of Unintended Consequences

In this world without rules, the monkeys would surely stumble upon a principle that economists call the "Law of Unintended Consequences." As they peck away, creating an infinite number of economic theories, many of them will be absurd or even self-defeating. Yet, some might just work.

## The Principle of Survival of the Fittest Theory

Just as in nature, only the most effective theories would survive. Over time, the monkeys' typewriters would fill with theories that solve real economic problems—or at least appear to do so. As we all know, a good enough theory can often pass for a great one, especially when it delivers results.

> "A wise monkey knows that a good enough theory is better than no theory at all."

## The Theory of the Infinite Monkey Cage

Of course, not every economic problem can be solved by a monkey with a typewriter. Some issues require human ingenuity and understanding. But our simian scribes might just stumble upon something profound: the idea that even the most complex problems can be tackled by many minds working together, each contributing their unique insights.

## The Bottom Line

In this thought experiment, we've imagined a world without economic rules and let loose an army of monkeys with typewriters. It's a ridiculous scenario, but it reveals something important: the power of collective intellect to solve complex problems. And while we may not want to leave economics in the hands of our simian counterparts, we can take inspiration from their determination and their ability to stumble upon solutions through sheer persistence.
